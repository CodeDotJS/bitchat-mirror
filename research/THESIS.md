# Thesis — what `/why` is for

## One sentence

**India’s government treats the public internet as a privilege it can revoke, and communication tools as something it is entitled to disable when they escape that revoke button — this page documents that record with sources, then explains why an independent APK mirror exists.**

## What this page is not

- Not a bitchat marketing page
- Not a soft “we believe in free speech” pamphlet
- Not a Wikipedia dump with pretty cards
- Not centered on Jack Dorsey, Bluetooth, or product features

## What this page is

A **brutal, sourced information experience** about:

1. **Routine internet shutdowns** as administrative practice in India  
2. **Scale** — cumulative and annual figures from Access Now / #KeepItOn  
3. **Duration** — J&K 552 days; Manipur ~212 days  
4. **Cost** — economic estimates (Top10VPN, ICRIER) as secondary harm evidence  
5. **Legal theatre vs practice** — *Anuradha Bhasin* proportionality requirements vs continued blackouts  
6. **Intermediary compulsion** — Sahyog / §79(3)(b) as faster takedown machinery  
7. **Entitlement over offline tools** — July 2026 I4C order against bitchat *because* it works during shutdowns  

bitchat appears at the **end of the chain**: when the state cuts the network *and* tries to erase the install path for tools that survive the cut, mirroring binaries is continuity infrastructure.

## Emotional / editorial goal

Readers should finish the page feeling:

- informed (every number clickable to a source)  
- unsettled (the pattern is systematic, not episodic)  
- clear on agency (install / mirror / share before the blackout)

## Voice

Direct. Specific. Sourced. No euphemism for “communications restrictions” when the tracker calls them shutdowns. No false balance that invents a debate where the data is one-sided on frequency.
