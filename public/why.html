Why this exists

Communication tools should not disappear because a government or platform decides they are inconvenient. This mirror keeps bitchat’s Android builds reachable when the usual download paths get blocked or go dark.

The stance

bitchat is built for peer-to-peer mesh over Bluetooth — so people can still message nearby when the wider internet is filtered, throttled, or switched off. Centralised app stores and cloud chat make silencing easy. A BLE mesh does not. When the cell tower is dark, nearby phones can still pass messages hop by hop.

Mirroring APKs is a small act of infrastructure: if one download link dies, another copy remains. Install the app while you can. Share the file. Keep the source commit in sight.

The context

India · July 2026

Why this mirror exists now

On 23 July 2026, the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) under the Ministry of Home Affairs ordered GitHub to disable access to bitchat repositories within three hours — citing that the app’s Bluetooth mesh design lets people communicate without the internet, registration, or central logs, and therefore “impedes lawful interception” during unrest and internet suspensions. Jack Dorsey published the notice: “The government of India does not like technologies like Bitchat and wants it taken down.” The order landed while authorities were already suspending mobile data around student protests in central Delhi.

That is the point of this site. When the state cuts the network and tries to erase the install path, an independent APK mirror is not a convenience — it is continuity. bitchat only helps if you can still get the binary.

India’s share of global shutdowns

920 / 2,102

Access Now’s #KeepItOn project has logged 2,102 deliberate internet shutdowns worldwide since 2016. India alone accounts for 920 — more than any other country in the dataset.

Still #1 among democracies

65 in 2025

India imposed 65 shutdowns in 2025 and 84 in 2024 — the highest totals in any democracy those years. Triggers include protests, communal violence, conflict, and religious holidays across a dozen-plus states.

Longest democratic blackout

552 days

After August 2019 in Jammu & Kashmir, people lived through months of no connectivity and then prolonged no-to-low (2G-only) access. Full 4G returned only in February 2021 — among the longest communications restrictions ever recorded in a democracy.

Internet freedom score

51 / 100

Freedom House’s Freedom on the Net 2025 still rates India “Partly Free.” Short, localised shutdowns remain a routine tool of administration — not a rare emergency measure.

The pattern

Indian authorities do not only switch off mobile data. They also pressure the platforms that host speech, software, and distribution. The Home Ministry’s Sahyog portal (rolled out October 2024) lets central and state agencies push takedown notices to intermediaries under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act — a faster, less safeguarded path than the older Section 69A blocking process. Platforms from Google and Telegram to Apple have been onboarded; X challenged Sahyog in court as a “censorship portal” and lost in the Karnataka High Court in 2025.

Targeting bitchat’s GitHub repos extends that machinery from posts and videos to source code and installable software — specifically because the app is designed to keep working when the cell network is dark. FireChat and Bridgefy filled a similar role for protesters in Hong Kong; bitchat is the same class of tool. If you wait until the blackout to look for an APK, you are already too late.

This mirror keeps signed Android builds reachable, tagged to their upstream commits, outside a single corporate download funnel that can be ordered to go dark in three hours.
