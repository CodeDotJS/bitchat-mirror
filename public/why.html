We do not believe in internet censorship

The state treats the internet as a switch it owns

India has made deliberate internet shutdowns ordinary. It has built faster ways to compel platforms to erase speech and software. And when a tool still works after the network is cut, it has tried to erase the install path too. This page is the record — with sources. The mirror is what you do about the last part.

These are not outages. Access Now’s #KeepItOn project counts deliberate shutdowns — ordered cuts. [5] Local and state governments use them during protests, communal violence, conflict, and religious holidays: exactly when people need to reach each other. [4] [6]

Entitlement over communication

Law on paper · power in practice

The Court drew a line. Shutdowns continued.

In Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020), the Supreme Court held that freedom of speech and expression, and the freedom to practise trade or business, using the medium of the internet, are protected under Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). Restrictions must meet reasonableness and proportionality. Indefinite suspension under the Suspension Rules is not permitted. [12]

The Court did not invent a free-standing “right to internet access.” Even so: after that judgment, India kept leading the world in shutdown counts — 116 in 2023, 84 in 2024, 65 in 2025. [9] [6] [4] Proportionality on paper; blackouts as habit.

Then they automated the takedown channel

Cutting mobile data is only one lever. The other is compelling the companies that host speech, apps, and code. In October 2024 the Home Ministry’s Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre launched Sahyog — a portal that expedites Section 79(3)(b) notices to intermediaries under the IT Act: a faster path than the older, more procedurally constrained Section 69A blocking process. [13]

RTI data showed 130 Sahyog notices between October 2024 and early April 2025. In related disclosures, I4C cited 426 §79(3)(b) notices covering over one lakh pieces of content in a broader year-long window. [14] X Corp called Sahyog a “censorship portal.” The Karnataka High Court upheld it in 2025. [15]

A pattern, not a moment

2019 → 2026
  1. Aug 2019 – Feb 2021
    Jammu & Kashmir goes dark, then dim. After Article 370’s abrogation: weeks of near-total blackout, then prolonged no-to-low (2G) access. Full 4G returned after 552 days — among the longest such restrictions in any democracy. [7] [8]
  2. 10 Jan 2020
    Supreme Court sets proportionality rules. Anuradha Bhasin: speech and trade via the internet are constitutionally protected; indefinite suspension is not. [12]
  3. 2023
    Manipur: over two hundred days. Statewide disruption from May through December (~212 days), hitting roughly 3.2 million people — while India posts 116 shutdowns nationally. [9] Estimated economic loss from major Indian restrictions that year: $585.4 million. [10]
  4. Oct 2024 → 2025
    Sahyog: takedowns at administrative speed. Portal live. Notices flow. Court challenge fails. Intermediaries — including code hosts — sit inside a compliance machine designed for speed, not contestation. [13] [14] [15]
  5. 23 Jul 2026
    The switch is not enough — so they target what survives it. I4C orders GitHub to disable repositories for an offline Bluetooth mesh messenger within three hours, because it lets people communicate without the internet the state just suspended. [1] [2]

When offline tools become the target

23 July 2026 · I4C
Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre IT Act §79(3)(b) · three-hour deadline

GitHub ordered to disable bitchat — because it works during shutdowns

This is not a footnote about an app. It is the logical end of the pattern above. After years of cutting connectivity, the state confronted a tool that routes around the cut — peer-to-peer Bluetooth mesh, no accounts, no central logs — and reached for the distribution channel instead. [1] [2] The order landed while mobile data around Delhi student protests was already being suspended. [1]

“The government of India does not like technologies like Bitchat and wants it taken down.”

— Jack Dorsey, publishing the I4C notice [1]

What the notice itself argued

  • Anonymous mesh chat without registration or central logs “significantly impedes lawful interception, attribution, and investigation.” [2]
  • The architecture could keep people communicating during internet shutdowns imposed by authorities. [2] [3]
  • GitHub had three hours to comply — or risk losing intermediary safe-harbour under Rule 7 of the IT Rules, 2021. [3]

Read the rationale twice. The grievance is not a specific crime inside a binary. The grievance is survival of communication after the state flips the network switch. That is entitlement: over the tower, over the platform, and over the last hop between two phones.

Why a mirror

Mesh messaging only matters if you can still obtain the binary. GitHub, app stores, and CDNs are single points of failure under §79(3)(b). This site keeps signed Android builds, checksums, and tagged source commits reachable outside a funnel that can be ordered dark in three hours.

Install before you need it. Verify the hash. Share the file offline. The blackout is the worst time to start looking.
