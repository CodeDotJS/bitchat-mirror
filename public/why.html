We do not believe in internet censorship

Why this exists

On 23 July 2026, Indian authorities ordered GitHub to disable bitchat — an offline Bluetooth mesh messenger — within three hours. [1] This mirror exists so the Android builds remain reachable when the install path is ordered dark.

The trigger

23 July 2026
I4C · Ministry of Home Affairs IT Act §79(3)(b) · IT Rules 2021

GitHub ordered to disable bitchat repositories in three hours

The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) directed GitHub to remove or disable access to repositories hosting bitchat — a peer-to-peer messenger that relays encrypted messages over Bluetooth mesh without mobile networks, internet, accounts, or central servers. [1] [2] The order arrived while authorities were already suspending mobile data around student protests at Jantar Mantar in central Delhi. [1]

“The government of India does not like technologies like Bitchat and wants it taken down.”

— Jack Dorsey, publishing the I4C notice [1]

What the notice argued

  • Anonymous mesh chat without registration, phone numbers, or central logs “significantly impedes lawful interception, attribution, and investigation.” [2]
  • The architecture could be used to keep communicating during internet shutdowns and to coordinate assemblies the state calls unlawful. [2] [3]
  • Non-compliance risks loss of intermediary safe-harbour under Rule 7 of the IT Rules, 2021. [3]

Read that carefully: the stated problem is not a specific crime in the binary — it is that the tool still works when the network is cut. Targeting the GitHub install path is how you disable a mesh app without owning Bluetooth.

How we got here

A short timeline
  1. Aug 2019 – Feb 2021
    Jammu & Kashmir communications blockade. After the abrogation of Article 370, the region faced weeks of near-total blackout, then prolonged 2G-only / restricted access. Full 4G returned after 552 days of no-to-low connectivity. [7] [8]
  2. 10 Jan 2020
    Supreme Court, Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India. The Court held that freedom of speech and expression, and the freedom to practise trade or business, using the medium of the internet, are constitutionally protected under Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). Restrictions must meet reasonableness and proportionality; indefinite suspension under the Suspension Rules is not permitted. The Court did not create a free-standing “right to internet access.” [12]
  3. 2023
    India leads the world in shutdowns — again. Access Now recorded at least 116 Indian shutdowns. In Manipur, a statewide disruption ran ~212 days (May–December), with roughly 3.2 million people affected. [9]
  4. Oct 2024
    Sahyog portal goes live. The Home Ministry’s I4C launches Sahyog to automate Section 79(3)(b) takedown notices to intermediaries — a faster channel than the older, more procedurally constrained Section 69A blocking route. RTI data later showed 130 Sahyog notices between October 2024 and early April 2025; separately, I4C told court it sent 426 §79(3)(b) notices covering over 1 lakh pieces of content in a year. [13] [14]
  5. 2024–2025
    X challenges Sahyog; Karnataka High Court upholds it. X Corp called Sahyog a “censorship portal,” arguing §79(3)(b) was being used to sidestep Section 69A safeguards. The Karnataka High Court dismissed the challenge in 2025 and treated Sahyog as a legitimate administrative channel — raising the compliance pressure on every intermediary, including code hosts. [15]
  6. Jul 2025
    bitchat ships. An open-source Bluetooth mesh + Nostr messenger — no accounts, no phone numbers, no central servers — built for when towers go dark. Earlier mesh apps (FireChat, Bridgefy) were used by protesters in Hong Kong for the same reason. [16] [17]
  7. 23 Jul 2026
    I4C → GitHub: disable bitchat. Three hours to comply. The notice’s core complaint: the app works during internet suspensions. [1] [2]
  8. Now
    This mirror. Independent APK hosting, checksums, and tagged source commits — outside a single funnel that can be ordered offline before dinner.

The argument

Shutdowns are policy, not accidents

Access Now’s #KeepItOn project has logged 2,102 deliberate shutdowns worldwide since 2016; India alone accounts for 920. [4] These are usually local or state-level — not a single national kill switch — but they recur during protests, communal tension, conflict, and religious holidays, exactly when people most need to reach family, journalists, and mutual aid. [5] [6]

The economic damage is not theoretical. Top10VPN estimated $585.4 million in losses from major Indian restrictions in 2023, with Manipur’s months-long blackout dominating the hours count. [10] Earlier ICRIER work put the 2012–2017 bill near $3 billion. [18]

Mesh is the rational response

When the cell network is the control surface, the workaround is physical proximity: phones talking to phones. bitchat’s BLE mesh relays messages hop by hop without a tower, an account, or a company that can be served a notice. [16] That is why protesters have reached for FireChat and Bridgefy before, and why authorities now reach for the distribution channel. [17]

A mesh app you cannot install is useless. GitHub, Play Store, and cloud CDNs are single points of failure under §79(3)(b). Mirroring signed APKs with visible SHA-256 checksums and upstream commit tags is boring infrastructure — and that is the point.

Clarity

This site is unofficial and not affiliated with bitchat’s upstream maintainers. It does not claim that every shutdown is unlawful, or that every takedown is unconstitutional. It documents a pattern — routine blackouts, expanding intermediary compulsion, and a direct strike on offline messaging code — and keeps the binaries reachable so users can decide for themselves.

What you can do

Install while the link still resolves. Verify the checksum. Share the APK offline. Read the tagged source. The moment you need mesh is the moment you cannot download it.
